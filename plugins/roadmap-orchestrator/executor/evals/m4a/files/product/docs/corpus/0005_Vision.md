# Tidewater: what we are building and why

*Owner: the harbour master. Read this first; everything else in this folder serves it.*

## Purpose

Skippers book a berth for a tide window without phoning the harbour office.

The harbour is small and tidal. Boats can only come over the sill for an hour and a half either side of high
water, and on a busy weekend the six berths go fast. Today the harbour office takes bookings by phone and keeps
them in a paper diary. Calls come in at all hours, the diary is only as good as whoever wrote in it last, and
every season we have at least one evening where two boats arrive for the same berth.

Tidewater replaces the phone and the diary. A skipper books from a phone or a laptop, sees which berths are free
for which windows, and gets on with the passage.

## What we will not give up

A berth is never promised to two vessels for the same tide window.

This one is not negotiable. A double booking on a falling tide is not an inconvenience: the second boat has
nowhere safe to go until the next tide, and the harbour carries that risk. If Tidewater ever has to choose
between taking a booking and keeping this promise, it keeps the promise and says no.

## How we trade things off

When in doubt, a plain refusal now beats a surprise on the quay. Tidewater may turn a skipper away; it must
never let one find out on arrival that the booking was not what they thought.

## The harbour we want

These are the scenes we are working towards. Each is a picture of the harbour working well, not a feature list.

**Coming in on the evening tide.** A skipper heading home on the evening tide has booked ahead. A text on her
phone says which berth is hers and for which window. When she comes over the sill, the berth is empty and
waiting.

**When plans change.** A skipper whose plans change cancels in good time, from the quay or from home, and the
berth goes back to the pool for someone else. A cancellation is as clear to the skipper as a booking: they get
told it went through, and nobody is left wondering.

**The harbour master's morning.** The harbour master starts the day with one view of today's tides, the berths
and who is coming in on each window, including anything cancelled overnight. No diary, no phone log.

**Visiting yachts.** A visiting yacht that has never been here before can find out which windows its draught
allows, book a berth that fits, and arrive knowing the depth it will sit in.

## What is out of scope for now

Payments, mooring fees and the winter lay-up list stay in the harbour office's accounts system. Tidewater does
not replace the VHF watch: anything urgent still goes over the radio.
