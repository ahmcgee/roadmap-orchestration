# Operations

Running Tidewater day to day at the harbour office.

## The office machine

Tidewater runs on the office machine, a small desktop in the harbour office, and on the kiosk on the quay, which
uses the office machine's shared folder for the ledger. The machine is on all the time during the season and
switched off in the winter months except for the weekly check.

## Daily routine

1. In the morning, the office prints `tidewater list` for the day and pins it up by the radio.
2. During the day, the office answers questions at the kiosk and on the phone and books on behalf of skippers who
   ring in.
3. In the evening, the harbour master checks the next day's list against the berth register and phones anyone
   whose boat looks too deep for the berth they booked.

## Backups

The booking ledger is backed up every night to the network drive in the harbour office. The backup keeps thirty
nights. Once a month the harbour master copies the latest backup to a memory stick that goes home with her.

## When something goes wrong

- **A clash on the quay.** Two boats for one berth. The harbour master puts the later one where there is room and
  tells us how it happened, so we can find out why Tidewater let it through.
- **The kiosk is down.** Skippers phone the office as they used to; the office books on the office machine.
- **The ledger will not load.** Restore last night's backup and re-enter the day's bookings from the printed list.

## Winter

In the winter the harbour takes few visitors and bookings drop to a handful a week. That is when the tide table
for the next year is typed in and checked, and when changes to Tidewater are made and tried out before the season.

## Who to call

The harbour master runs Tidewater. The office staff can use every command. Changes to the code go through the
harbour master, who keeps this folder up to date with them.
